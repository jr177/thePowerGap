About the book

Marriage, it turns out, is a mirror.

The world projects its power dynamics onto a marriage. In an interracial one, you can watch it happen.

The Power Gap is a first-person memoir and case study about the credibility, ownership, and leadership gap between women of color and white men. Interracial relationships make that gap visible: who is believed, who is assumed to own the property, and who is seen as the leader.

The book follows the author's marriage and divorce through four courts and draws on conversations with more than fifty interracial couples, along with legal experts, psychologists, and sociologists. It shows how institutions, from banks to courtrooms, quietly perpetuate unfairness, and what women of color can do about it.

The author has written open letters to the courts she has appeared before. Excerpts follow.

“All in the hope that one day, my pain will be valued the same as my ex-husband's.

My voice will be just as loud and garner the same response and attention.

Open letter to a California family court, July 2024
Open letter to a California appellate court, January 2026
“The Power Gap [is] the structural phenomenon in which individuals with social and institutional privilege, including racial and gender privilege, receive the benefit of doubt, the benefit of omission, and the benefit of procedural grace, while those without such privilege must prove and re-prove every fact, every dollar, every step.”
“All I ask—all I have ever asked—is to be treated equally under the law. Not advantage. Not privilege. Simply equality.”
Open letter to a California family court, July 2024
“As a woman of color, I am held to an exceedingly high standard of scrutiny. It's like the porchlight is always shining brightly on me. I can have nothing and still there will be scrutiny – because I might get away with something. Anything. The default is that I am not trustworthy – that I am not worthy.”

How the book is organized

Three parts: the problem, the effects, the way forward.

I
Part one

Why the gap exists

The societal power differential explained, and how it shows up inside an interracial relationship, told through the author's own story.

II
Part two

What the gap does

How it shapes the lives of women of color and white men, in institutions and in relationships, from property ownership to the complexities of interracial marriage.

III
Part three

How to close it

Conversations with couples and experts, plus practical recommendations for strengthening relationships and empowering women of color, in institutions and in private life.

About the author

Joanne Rodrigues

Joanne Rodrigues is a data scientist, author, and public policy leader working at the intersection of AI, demographics, and equitable technology. She holds degrees in political science and demography from UC Berkeley, applicable mathematics from the London School of Economics, and international political economy from Georgetown.

She founded Clinic Price Check, a healthcare transparency company and 2020 TechCrunch Battlefield SF finalist, and has led analytics teams in Silicon Valley.

Also by Joanne Rodrigues: Product Analytics (Addison-Wesley) and Demographic Techniques for Data Scientists (Springer Nature, forthcoming).
